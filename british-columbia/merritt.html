Flooring options and costs in Merritt

Merritt homeowners usually start with comfort and style, then quickly run into the real driver of total cost: moisture management. With 59.3% of homes in the community built before 1981, it’s common to see older crawlspaces, dated subfloors, and uneven surfaces that need levelling, grinding, or patching before flooring goes down. In many Thompson–Okanagan houses, that prep step can add several dollars per square foot, and it’s one reason quotes can feel higher than expected when comparing to big-city pricing. (Statistics Canada, 2021 Census).

In the Thompson–Okanagan, the combination of dry winters and hot, sometimes smoky summers creates noticeable indoor humidity swings. Those swings matter most for wood: solid hardwood expands and contracts with moisture and temperature, so it’s usually limited to above-grade installations. Below-grade areas and basements bring additional risk from vapour and occasional spills—so local contractors typically recommend engineered hardwood or luxury vinyl plank (LVP) there. Radiant heating is also more common in newer builds, which pushes many homeowners toward radiant-compatible engineered wood, tile, and quality LVP.

In Merritt, trade demand is especially steady in areas like the downtown core and along Highway 5 corridors, where lots of renovations are tied to older housing stock and seasonal occupancy. Permits & regulations in British Columbia

In British Columbia, installing flooring itself typically does not require a building permit. However, associated work can trigger permit requirements—especially when you’re dealing with electrical components or structural changes. For example, if you’re installing or modifying in-floor heating systems (including wiring and thermostats), that work often involves electrical permitting requirements. Likewise, if you uncover damage and need structural subfloor repair or other substantial changes, a permit may be required depending on what’s being altered. For many “swap floors on a stable subfloor” projects, you’re usually working under general trade rules without a permit.

For condo owners, the bigger hurdle is often not permits—it’s the building’s sound-transmission rules. Upper-floor installations using hardwood or tile commonly face restrictions around underlay thickness and impact-sound performance (STC/IIC targets). Before you order materials, review the condo declaration and request board approval in writing if the building’s rules require it—especially when installing hard surfaces.

To verify your Merritt contractor, start with their British Columbia licence information through the appropriate online registry or licensing listings, then request a certificate of insurance showing liability coverage. Ask for WSIB/WCB coverage evidence (clearance letter or current coverage confirmation, depending on the situation). A reputable installer should provide these documents before you sign, and they should match the name used on the quote and invoices.

Best flooring for each room in your Merritt home

Choosing the right flooring in Merritt is about matching the material to the room’s moisture risk, traffic, and comfort needs—then accounting for Thompson–Okanagan humidity swings. In living and dining rooms, engineered hardwood or high-quality laminate is a practical balance: you get a warm, “wood-like” look without the instability that can come with solid wood. For the master bedroom, carpet is still a favourite in Merritt for comfort and sound absorption, while engineered hardwood can work well if the subfloor is properly prepped and kept dry.

Kitchens take the most abuse day-to-day, so luxury vinyl plank (LVP) or large-format porcelain tile are the best performers. LVP offers waterproof protection from splashes and easy cleaning, while porcelain tile gives the longest wear and excellent resistance to water when installed over properly prepared surfaces. Basements (especially below-grade areas) are where LVP usually wins: fully waterproof products tolerate seasonal vapour better than solid wood and most laminate, which can swell at the seams if moisture finds its way in.

Bathrooms should be porcelain or ceramic tile only—this is one of those choices where cost pays back through longevity and easier maintenance. Radiant heat compatibility matters in rooms with in-floor heating circuits: engineered wood (rated for radiant) and tile are typically the safest bets, while many solid hardwood and some laminate products are limited or require specific installation conditions.

Example: if you’re comparing laminate in a main area, laminate might sit around the $4–$10 band per square foot, while LVP commonly lands closer to $6.50–$13.50 installed depending on product and prep. If that room borders the kitchen or you have busy foot traffic, the extra cost is often justified because LVP’s waterproof top layer reduces edge-risk and reduces the chance of moisture-related callbacks.

How to choose a flooring contractor in Merritt

Start with credentials that protect you in Merritt and across British Columbia. Ask for their British Columbia licensing information and confirm it matches the company name on the quote. Request a certificate of liability insurance—this is your safety net if materials or property are damaged during the job. For worker coverage, verify WSIB/WCB coverage with a clearance letter or current coverage proof (your contractor should be willing to provide it before work begins). If they can’t provide documentation, that’s a red flag.

Next, request 2–3 itemised written quotes rather than a single lump sum. A solid quote separates labour and materials and shows what’s included: prep work, underlay or membranes, adhesives (if glue-down), removal and disposal, and any levelling/grinding needed. Read the scope carefully for exclusions such as moving appliances, patching drywall, subfloor repair that exceeds a defined allowance, or disposal fees. Confirm whether permits (if required for related work like electrical) are included or handled by the contractor.

Warranty is critical: ask for the workmanship warranty length and whether it’s transferable if you sell the home. Also confirm the product manufacturer warranty terms (and what voids them). Keep payment practical: never pay more than about 10–15% upfront, and hold back payment until the job is complete and cleaned up.

Finally, insist on a written timeline with a start date and completion estimate, including a note about weather/indoor acclimation for wood and moisture-sensitive installs in Merritt’s seasonal swings.

Frequently asked questions — flooring in Merritt

Is cork or bamboo flooring a good choice in Merritt?

Cork and bamboo can be attractive, but in Merritt (and across the Thompson–Okanagan), the deciding factor is moisture tolerance and indoor humidity stability. Cork is naturally resilient and can handle minor impacts well, yet it’s still a “natural” material that can be affected by humidity swings if the subfloor isn’t well-prepped or if vapour comes up from below grade. Bamboo performance depends heavily on whether it’s engineered (often the safer direction) versus solid, and whether it’s installed correctly with the right underlayment and acclimation. In older homes built before 1981, Merritt subfloors often need levelling or repairs first, which can change the cost and the risk profile. If you’re considering these materials, compare total installed cost against engineered hardwood or LVP; in many cases, LVP in the $6.50–$13.50 band gives more moisture forgiveness for less stress.

What is the difference between floating and glued-down flooring?

Floating flooring is installed without permanent attachment to the subfloor. For click-lock laminate and many engineered systems, the boards interlock and rest over an underlayment or pad; the goal is to allow slight movement with seasonal expansion. Glued-down flooring is fixed to the subfloor using approved adhesives, which can increase stability and improve sound control in some setups, but it also requires stricter subfloor prep and correct moisture conditions—especially on concrete. In Merritt’s climate with dry winters and humid summer swings, floating installs are often chosen for above-grade areas because they tolerate movement better. For basements or below-grade moisture management, contractors frequently steer homeowners toward LVP (waterproof) or carefully specified engineered options. If you have radiant heat, make sure the product is rated for it and that the installation method matches the manufacturer’s requirements.

What flooring holds up best to pets in a Merritt home?

For pets in Merritt, durability is about more than scratch resistance—it’s also moisture tolerance and ease of cleanup. Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) usually performs best because the wear layer is designed to resist scratches and it’s waterproof, which matters if accidents happen or if there’s seasonal condensation near doors. High-quality engineered hardwood can work too, but it must be installed correctly on a stable, well-prepped above-grade subfloor to avoid problems from moisture swings. Laminate is decent for pets if the household keeps spills under control, but it’s more vulnerable at seams and edges. Carpet is comfortable for pets, yet it can hold odours and moisture if stains aren’t addressed quickly. If you want the most forgiving option, LVP in the $6.50–$13.50 installed range is a common sweet spot in Merritt because it balances pet practicality with a clean, modern look.

How do I fix squeaky floors in my Merritt home?

Squeaks are usually a subfloor movement issue, not a “surface” problem, so fixing them after the new flooring is installed can be expensive. If you’re planning flooring in Merritt, ask your contractor to identify the squeak source during the pre-install walk: common causes include loose subfloor fasteners, worn tongue-and-groove, or insufficient joist spacing. In older homes built before 1981, it’s also common to find sections that were patched during past renovations, where fastening and flatness vary. The remedy is typically to re-secure subfloor panels (screw or nail from above/through as appropriate), add blocking where needed, and ensure the floor is flat before underlayment or flooring goes down. A proper contractor should include these checks in the scope, and if repairs exceed an allowance, they should show you the added cost before proceeding.

What is luxury vinyl plank and is it worth it in Merritt?

Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) is a synthetic flooring with a wear layer on top and a cushioned or rigid backing system underneath. In Merritt and the Thompson–Okanagan, it’s often worth it because it tolerates moisture better than solid hardwood and most laminate—an important advantage with humidity swings, crawlspaces, and many homes that include basements. LVP is also easier to maintain: spills clean up with minimal risk to the core, and it’s a great option in kitchens and finished basements where water events are more likely. Cost-wise, LVP commonly lands around the $6.50–$13.50 installed band, which can be less than tile while still offering better moisture forgiveness than laminate. If you want long-term durability without worrying as much about vapour or seasonal expansion, LVP is one of the most dependable choices we see locally.
