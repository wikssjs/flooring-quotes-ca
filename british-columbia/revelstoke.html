Flooring options and costs in Revelstoke

Revelstoke homeowners typically feel flooring pricing sooner than they expect because the town sits in a climate that swings from dry winters to hot, sometimes smoky summers. In practice, that means indoor humidity can change noticeably through the year, and older houses often need more prep work than newer builds. With 66.4% of homes built before 1981 in the local profile, it’s common to find uneven subfloors, patched renovations, or older OSB/plank that needs levelling or repair before you can install new finishes. For comparison shopping, those prep steps can add roughly $1–$4 per sq ft, and sometimes more, especially when tile or engineered wood demands tighter flatness.

In the Thompson–Okanagan region, moisture management is a major cost driver—particularly in basements, crawlspaces, and any on-grade areas that see seasonal swings. Solid hardwood is sensitive to expansion and contraction, so most contractors steer you away from solid wood in below-grade spaces. Engineered hardwood and luxury vinyl plank (LVP) are usually better suited because engineered cores and vinyl’s synthetic construction tolerate seasonal movement, minor slab moisture, and everyday spills more consistently. Where radiant heating is present (common in colder pockets and newer builds), many solid hardwood products are less compatible, while engineered wood, quality LVP, and tile are often easier to match to the heating system.

Trade demand is especially steady in older neighbourhoods and renovation-heavy pockets such as downtown/heritage areas and the newer edges of town where basements and crawlspaces are frequent. Permits & regulations in British Columbia

In British Columbia, flooring installation itself usually does not require a building permit. That said, associated work can trigger permits or other regulatory steps—especially where you touch electrical systems, structural elements, or in-floor heating circuits. For example, if your project involves repairing or replacing a subfloor in a way that changes structural components, addressing rot/damage that affects framing, or modifying existing radiant heat electrical components, you may need the proper permits before work starts. Similarly, if you’re adding a new heated floor system, the electrical portion typically needs to be handled under the applicable electrical permitting process.

In multi-unit buildings (condominiums), the practical “rules” often come from the corporation rather than a building permit. Many condo bylaws and declarations restrict hard flooring on upper floors due to sound transmission. That can mean you must use specific underlay systems and sometimes meet performance expectations related to impact and airborne sound. Always review the condo declaration and request board approval in writing before installing hardwood or tile above grade in a unit.

To verify a contractor in Revelstoke: confirm their British Columbia licence/registration status (where applicable for their trade scope), ask for a current certificate of liability insurance, and request proof of workers’ compensation coverage (WCB/WSIB coverage as applicable). You should also ask whether they can provide a clearance letter or equivalent proof before starting, and you can cross-check insurance documents for validity dates and correct business name.

Best flooring for each room in your Revelstoke home

Picking the right flooring in Revelstoke is mostly about matching material behaviour to moisture, traffic, and comfort in a home where humidity can swing through the year. For living and dining areas, engineered hardwood or high-quality laminate often hits the sweet spot: they add warmth and look like wood, while being more forgiving than solid hardwood when humidity changes. For bedrooms, carpet is still a favourite because it softens sound, improves comfort underfoot, and helps with cold winter floors—especially in rooms that don’t see heavy moisture exposure.

For the kitchen, I usually steer homeowners toward luxury vinyl plank (LVP) or large-format porcelain tile. LVP handles everyday water and spills without the same expansion concerns as wood, and tile is extremely durable when installed with a proper underlayment system. In basements (finished), LVP is typically the clear winner in the Thompson–Okanagan because it’s waterproof and tolerates the moisture vapour risk that often exists below grade. Solid wood and most laminate products are more likely to struggle in these conditions.

Bathrooms should be porcelain or ceramic tile only, due to the moisture exposure. Where radiant heating is present, pay attention to compatibility: engineered wood, tile, and properly rated LVP are commonly used in British Columbia new construction and renovations, while many solid hardwood products need stricter humidity control and may not be recommended over radiant systems.

As a cost example, if you compare tile versus LVP in a basement rec room, tile prices commonly fall in the $15–$30 per sq ft installed range, while LVP often lands closer to $7–$14 installed. You might justify tile if you need maximum stiffness and a “forever” surface—but in a moisture-prone basement, LVP’s stability and lower risk often make the premium worth it only when the entire room benefits from tile’s properties.

How to choose a flooring contractor in Revelstoke

When you hire a flooring contractor in British Columbia (including Revelstoke), start by verifying they’re set up to work safely and legally. Ask for their proof of current liability insurance—look for the correct business name and coverage limits—and confirm their workers’ compensation coverage (WCB/WSIB coverage as applicable). If their trade scope requires specific registration or licensing for their activities, request the current licence/registration details before work begins.

Next, get 2–3 itemised written quotes. A solid quote separates labour and materials (including underlayment, membranes, adhesives, fasteners, transitions, and stair components). Avoid “lump sum” pricing that doesn’t explain what’s included. Read the scope carefully: is removal and disposal included? Are moulding/trim adjustments included? Is the quote including permit pull (if needed for electrical/radiant work) or electrician coordination? For workmanship, ask for the warranty terms in writing, including duration and what’s covered (installation defects vs product issues). Also confirm whether the manufacturer warranty is tied to installation by an approved contractor and whether it’s transferable if you sell.

For payment schedule, protect yourself: never pay more than about 10–15% upfront, and hold back until the job is complete and you’ve had a chance to inspect it. Finally, get a start date and completion estimate in writing, along with a plan for material delivery—Revelstoke projects often depend on lead times for specific planks/tiles and matching stair parts.

Frequently asked questions — flooring in Revelstoke

Can I install flooring over existing tile in Revelstoke?

Sometimes, but it depends on the tile surface condition and the type of new flooring you’re installing. In Revelstoke basements and main floors, we often see older tile that’s cracked, uneven, or slightly lifted at edges after years of movement. If the tile is stable and flat, some products—like certain LVP systems—can be installed over specific prepared substrates, but the floor still needs proper cleaning and assessment. For engineered wood or laminate, many installers require a smooth, flat base and may still prefer grinding/patching or a suitable underlayment solution.

Expect the contractor to measure flatness and discuss whether self-levelling is needed. If you’re comparing costs, remember that tile removal can push you into higher installed ranges (tile often sits around $15–$30 per sq ft), while LVP is commonly closer to $7–$14 per sq ft when your substrate preparation is straightforward.

What is the best flooring for a high-traffic area in Revelstoke?

For high-traffic areas in Revelstoke—entries, hallways, and main living zones—LVP and tile are usually the most trouble-free options. The Thompson–Okanagan’s humidity swings and occasional wet/dirty traffic (boots, snow-melt, and summer smoke haze) reward surfaces that tolerate spills and frequent cleaning. Quality LVP is waterproof and holds up well to daily scuffs, while porcelain/ceramic tile is extremely durable if installed with the correct underlayment system and proper grout/jointing.

If you want the look of wood, engineered hardwood can perform very well above grade, but it still benefits from careful mat placement and fast spill response. For a practical benchmark, if you’re choosing between tile at roughly $15–$30 per sq ft and LVP at around $7–$14 per sq ft, LVP often wins for cost and ease—while tile usually wins for the “most durable surface” goal.

How do I prepare my subfloor before new flooring?

Preparation is where the success or failure usually happens in Revelstoke homes, especially the older stock. Most installers start by checking flatness (not just “looks good”), then inspecting for squeaks, soft spots, and signs of moisture. For older 1970s–1990s renovations you often find patched areas, uneven plywood/OSB, or older underlayments that are compressing—these conditions need correction before installation.

Typical steps include cleaning debris, removing loose materials, repairing damaged subfloor sections, and levelling (grinding, patching, or self-levelling underlayment). In below-grade areas, contractors commonly use moisture mitigation products like primers and membranes before installing moisture-tolerant finishes (often LVP or tile). If your home is one of the older builds (local profile shows 66.4% built before 1981), it’s reasonable to budget for added levelling/repair—often $1–$4 per sq ft depending on what’s found.

Is cork or bamboo flooring a good choice in Revelstoke?

Cork can be a comfortable option underfoot and it’s generally more forgiving on noise, but in Revelstoke the decision comes down to moisture stability. Cork is typically best where moisture risk is low (above grade). If you’re considering cork in basements or any area with known humidity swings, it can be a tougher sell than LVP or tile because water/vapour exposure is the enemy.

Bamboo can look great and is often sold as strand-woven material, but it still has performance limits similar to other wood-based products: it’s sensitive to seasonal movement. In a region with dry winters and hotter, sometimes smoky summers, that seasonal cycling matters.

If you want “natural” with lower risk, many homeowners in British Columbia choose engineered wood (or LVP with a natural look). For basements, I still recommend waterproof LVP over cork/bamboo. For pricing context, LVP commonly sits around $7–$14 per sq ft installed, while engineered wood is often higher at roughly $13–$26 per sq ft.

What is the difference between floating and glued-down flooring?

Floating flooring is installed without permanent attachment to the subfloor. Most click-lock laminate and many engineered systems “float” over a prepared underlayment, expanding and contracting slightly across the room. This can make it easier to install on some subfloors, but it still requires correct flatness and the right underlayment selection. Floating floors also rely on good perimeter gaps to prevent buckling.

Glued-down flooring is permanently bonded to the subfloor using an adhesive. This method can reduce minor movement and may be preferred for certain engineered products or when you need additional stability—especially on challenging subfloors. It also makes substrate prep more critical because any contamination or unevenness can telegraph through or cause bond failures.

In Revelstoke, the moisture story matters: below-grade areas and slab concerns often push homeowners toward waterproof LVP or tile, while engineered hardwood selection and installation method should be matched to your site conditions. That’s why quotes can vary by 30–50% even for similar square footage.

What flooring holds up best to pets in a Revelstoke home?

For pets in Revelstoke, LVP and tile are usually the best-performing categories. Waterproof LVP is forgiving for accidents and easier to clean quickly, which matters when you have humidity swings and you don’t want liquids sitting on seams. It also tends to handle paw traffic and minor impacts better than carpet. Tile is extremely scratch-resistant, though dropping food bowls or toys can chip grout edges if the installation wasn’t done correctly.

Engineered hardwood can work for pets if it’s installed properly above grade, but it’s less forgiving for prolonged moisture exposure from urine or repeated spills. Carpet can be comfortable, but it’s harder to keep sanitary and dry if accidents happen; drying time and odour remediation can become expensive.

For cost planning, many pet households pick LVP around $7–$14 per sq ft installed as a balance of durability and risk reduction. If you’re going tile in a kitchen or main corridor, plan closer to $15–$30 per sq ft and budget for proper transitions and grout detailing.
